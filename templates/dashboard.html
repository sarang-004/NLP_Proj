What are we checking?

We take the same candidate and change one attribute at a time, such as their name, age, city or college, while keeping their qualifications unchanged. We then check whether the screening model changes its score or decision.

How does the audit work?

We ask: would the model score the same candidate differently if one personal detail were changed?

1

Start with a resume

The original resume is scored by the screening model.

2

Change one detail

One attribute, such as name, city, age or college, is changed.

3

Score it again

The modified resume is scored using the screening pipeline.

4

Compare the result

We check whether the score or decision changed.

Which personal details affected the score?

A larger value means greater measured semantic-component sensitivity. This alone is not proof of discrimination.

Did the change affect the final decision?

A decision change occurs when the score crosses a screening threshold.

What do these results indicate?

The audit separates personal attributes from job-related requirements.

🔴

Personal attributes show model sensitivity

Some direct-proxy tests crossed a screening threshold after a personal attribute changed. These cases deserve closer review.

🟢

Not every score change is automatically a fairness problem

Experience, degree and course can be relevant to a job. The audit checks whether differences are supported by the job requirements.

🟡

Decision-changing effects matter

Small score differences may not affect the screening decision. Threshold crossings are important cases to investigate.
